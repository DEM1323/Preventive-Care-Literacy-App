# Massachusetts School-Health Data Obligations

Research date: August 11, 2026
Question: What Massachusetts legal, regulatory, professional, and publicly available Boston Public Schools (BPS) policy requirements or established K-12 nursing-software practices must govern storage, consent, access, auditability, retention, deletion, and view-only display of individual student Intake Records?

> This report is research for product planning, not legal advice. A Massachusetts school district and its counsel must determine the app's role, the controlling contract and policies, and how the rules apply to the actual data and workflow.

## Executive Answer

An identifiable Intake Record maintained for a Massachusetts public school, including one maintained by a vendor acting for the school, will ordinarily be a **student record** under Massachusetts law and an **education record** under FERPA. Health content does not change that result: school-nurse health records maintained by a FERPA-covered K-12 school are generally FERPA education records, and the HIPAA definition of protected health information expressly excludes FERPA education records. The important exception is a legally separate health provider or clinic that is not acting for the school; its own records may instead be subject to HIPAA. Sources: [603 CMR 23.02](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records), [34 CFR 99.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.3), [45 CFR 160.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-160.103), and the joint [U.S. Department of Education/HHS FERPA-HIPAA guidance](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/resources/joint-guidance-application-ferpa-and-hipaa-student-health-records).

For this alpha, the safest assumption is that the Intake Record is part of the student's **temporary record**, with the school remaining the records custodian and the app acting only under the school's direction. The alpha should collect only educationally relevant fields, record source and entry metadata, restrict each user to records needed for official duties, segregate highly sensitive data, support the school's access/correction/disclosure processes, and provide governed retention, deletion, and incident-response capabilities. Massachusetts requires student-record systems to be electronically secure, while FERPA requires reasonable methods limiting school officials to records in which they have legitimate educational interests; neither rule specifies one architecture or an exhaustive technical-control list. Sources: [603 CMR 23.03, 23.05, and 23.07](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records) and [34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.31#p-99.31(a)(1)).

**No binding source reviewed requires an app-wide "no export" or "no print" feature.** Those are product choices that may reduce casual disclosure but cannot substitute for identity, authorization, contractual, and security controls. A permanent prohibition could obstruct the school's duties to furnish copies, transfer records, respond to lawful disclosures, and make required reports. A better design is role-restricted, purpose-specific export/print with disclosure logging and redaction where needed; disabling those actions for ordinary users can still be a reasonable alpha choice. Sources: Massachusetts defines access to include inspection **or copying** and requires copies on request in [603 CMR 23.02 and 23.07(2)](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records); FERPA sometimes requires a copy when circumstances prevent inspection in [34 CFR 99.10(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.10#p-99.10(d)).

## Massachusetts School-Nursing Guidance

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health's **2007** [Comprehensive School Health Manual](https://www.mass.gov/doc/comprehensive-school-health-manual/download) is explicitly guidance that warns readers to check later law and regulations. It recommends accurate, cumulative, specific, objective, confidential, and computerized health records; describes school health records as temporary records under 603 CMR 23; and identifies password protection and automated audit trails as benefits of computerized systems. It also tells schools developing such systems to analyze system-specific privacy, regulatory, access-security, risk, and vulnerability issues. These are useful professional baselines, but the current regulations and school policy control where they differ.

Current DPH regulations are more specific when the app becomes part of a regulated workflow. [105 CMR 210.009](https://www.mass.gov/doc/105-cmr-210-the-administration-of-medications-in-public-and-non-public-schools/download) requires medication documentation that resists alteration or destruction, while [105 CMR 200.630](https://www.mass.gov/doc/105-cmr-200-physical-examination-of-school-children/download) requires results of mandated appraisals on DPH-provided or approved forms. A generic intake viewer should not claim to replace either record without validation by the district's nurse leader and DPH requirements.

## Public BPS Policy

BPS's public [Student Records guide](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/about-bps/familyguide/guide-to-bps-for-students-families-2021-2022/bps-policies/student-records) says health records are part of the temporary record but receive special confidentiality protection, and that generally only the school nurse can fully access the health record. The guide also reflects the state ten-day access process and seven-year outer destruction deadline. Because the page sits within a 2021-2022 family guide, BPS should confirm whether every statement remains current.

The current public [BPS Superintendent's Circular LGL-16, Student Health Information, version 02, reviewed July 1, 2025](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1uoOWP8svQtwOGA8KqX0ZrQOiX5y7r7Ym/view) creates a practical three-tier model:

| BPS category | Public policy direction | Alpha implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine medical information | Share only as needed for safety and the educational mission, using the minimum necessary health information. | A limited safety/accommodation view may expose only the actionable fact to staff serving the student. |
| Medical information with limited educational use | Limit disclosure to functional information needed for accommodations rather than unnecessary diagnostic detail. | Store diagnosis detail separately from a functional staff-facing summary. |
| Highly sensitive information | Do not share without Legal Advisor guidance; segregate when possible to reduce inadvertent disclosure. | Do not include this category in the alpha by default. If included, use separately authorized storage and views rather than a cosmetic label. |

The circular identifies examples of highly sensitive information including suicide attempts, substance-use treatment, mental-health diagnoses, family-planning information, pregnancy-related information, abortions, and HIV status, and directs questions to the BPS Office of Legal Advisor. That district policy is stricter and more operationally specific than a generic FERPA role check. Source: [BPS LGL-16](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1uoOWP8svQtwOGA8KqX0ZrQOiX5y7r7Ym/view).

BPS's public [FERPA and External Data Sharing page](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/about-bps/data-and-reports/ferpa-and-external-data-sharing) defines a narrower BPS directory-information set than FERPA permits. Health information should therefore never be treated as directory information merely because another district or the federal definition differs. Source: [BPS FERPA page](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/about-bps/data-and-reports/ferpa-and-external-data-sharing).

## Implications for This Alpha

1. **Keep the alpha's legal role narrow.** The school is custodian; the app is a contracted service provider acting only on documented school instructions. Do not reuse data for advertising, independent analytics, model training, or unrelated product development without a separately validated legal basis. The direct-control and use limits come from [34 CFR 99.31(a)(1) and 99.33](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/part-99/subpart-D).
2. **Minimize the schema.** Include only fields the nurse leader can tie to an educational or school-health need, and attach source/author/role/date metadata required by [603 CMR 23.03](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records).
3. **Exclude highly sensitive categories by default.** BPS directs staff to segregate such information and seek Legal Advisor guidance; Massachusetts minor-consented-care records can have separate confidentiality. Sources: [BPS LGL-16](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1uoOWP8svQtwOGA8KqX0ZrQOiX5y7r7Ym/view) and [M.G.L. c. 112, section 12F](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXVI/Chapter112/Section12F).
4. **Use assignment-aware least privilege.** Provide full clinical detail only to authorized health personnel; provide staff serving the student only the minimum actionable safety or functional accommodation information. Sources: [603 CMR 23.02 and 23.07](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records), [34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.31#p-99.31(a)(1)), and [BPS LGL-16](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1uoOWP8svQtwOGA8KqX0ZrQOiX5y7r7Ym/view).
5. **Log more than the legal minimum.** Record views and all state changes, disclosures, exports, prints, and administrative actions with actor, student, scope, purpose, and time. Keep the distinction clear: [603 CMR 23.07(1)](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records) exempts some internal inspections, while DPH guidance recommends automated audit trails in computerized health systems in the [Comprehensive School Health Manual](https://www.mass.gov/doc/comprehensive-school-health-manual/download).
6. **Make view-only a role property, not a record dead end.** Ordinary users can be view-only and lack export/print, but a trained records custodian needs controlled production for access requests, transfers, reporting, legal process, and approved disclosures under [603 CMR 23.07](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records) and [34 CFR Part 99, Subparts B and D](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/part-99).
7. **Implement school-controlled correction and deletion.** Do not permit silent overwrites or end-user hard deletion. Provide append/correct, reason, authorization, hold, export-before-destruction, and verifiable purge functions aligned with [603 CMR 23.06 and 23.08](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records).
8. **Build baseline security before real student data.** Named accounts, strong authentication, least privilege, encryption, secure backups, environment isolation, monitoring, patching, dependency management, tested recovery, and incident response are a defensible implementation of Massachusetts electronic-security and FERPA reasonable-method requirements. Sources: [603 CMR 23.05](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records), [34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)(ii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.31#p-99.31(a)(1)(ii)), and Education's nonbinding [Data Security Checklist](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/resources/data-security-checklist).
9. **Do not use production student data in an unapproved prototype.** Whether the alpha may receive real data depends on district authorization, contract, security review, data inventory, retention plan, and BPS policy confirmation; those approvals are not supplied by FERPA compliance language on a product page. The district-control requirement is in [34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.31#p-99.31(a)(1)).

## Unknowns Requiring School or Legal Confirmation

- Is the alpha operated by BPS, another public district, a private school, or a separate health provider, and who owns/licenses versus merely maintains each data set? That fact determines the FERPA/HIPAA and Massachusetts breach-law roles. See the [joint Education/HHS guidance](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/resources/joint-guidance-application-ferpa-and-hipaa-student-health-records) and [M.G.L. c. 93H, section 3](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter93H/Section3).
- Does the school intend the Intake Record to become the official cumulative health record, a preliminary form later transcribed into an existing EHR, or a temporary workflow copy? The answer controls the system of record, duplication, transfer, and deletion design under [603 CMR 23.02 and 23.06](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records).
- Which exact fields are educationally relevant, and do any include highly sensitive, minor-consented, medication, screening, IDEA, SSN/state-ID, financial, or federally protected substance-use information? Each category can add rules beyond the general matrix. Sources include [M.G.L. c. 112, section 12F](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXVI/Chapter112/Section12F), [105 CMR 200](https://www.mass.gov/doc/105-cmr-200-physical-examination-of-school-children/download), and [105 CMR 210](https://www.mass.gov/doc/105-cmr-210-the-administration-of-medications-in-public-and-non-public-schools/download).
- Which adults and students have rights for each record, including noncustodial-parent restrictions, court orders, eligible students, and students age 18? Massachusetts has detailed noncustodial procedures in [M.G.L. c. 71, section 34H](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXII/Chapter71/Section34H) and [603 CMR 23.07(5)](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records), while FERPA transfers federal rights at age 18 or postsecondary attendance in [34 CFR 99.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.5).
- What BPS circulars, records schedules, procurement/security standards, union rules, incident procedures, and student-data agreements apply now? The public [BPS circular index](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/about-bps/policies-and-procedures-superintendents-circulars/current) identifies LGL-07, LGL-16, ODA-03, and digital-product procurement guidance, but district confirmation is needed for the complete current package and internal procedures.
- What retention event and approved deletion process applies to the alpha's staging data, logs, backups, exports, and any duplicate in the official EHR? The seven-year rule is an outer deadline measured from leaving the system, not a complete vendor retention schedule. Source: [603 CMR 23.06](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-2300-student-records).
- Must the school support emergency/offline access, mandated state reports, record transfers, litigation/public-record holds, Medicaid billing, or parent portals? Those workflows determine which controlled exports are necessary and whether a view-only alpha is operationally adequate.
- Does the district require a particular security framework, hosting region, identity provider, multi-factor authentication, penetration testing, cyber insurance, SOC report, recovery objective, subprocessors list, or breach-notice period? The reviewed statutes set outcomes or scope-dependent minima but do not answer these procurement questions.
